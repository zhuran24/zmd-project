# Independent-Derivation Comparison Rubric

Score Round 1 and Round 2 separately before comparing either with the current methodology. Preserve verbatim excerpts and label inferences as evaluator interpretations.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rule ownership | Rules are omitted or placed arbitrarily | Most rules have a location | Every hard rule has a justified enforcement and audit location |
| Rejection information | Only infeasible/unknown status | Informal reason or oversized witness | Sound, checkable, reusable explanation with stated scope |
| Escalation criterion | No criterion | Example-driven rule duplication | Explicit criterion based on repeated conflicts, cheap exact projection, or safe necessary conditions |
| Optimality evidence | Heuristic score | Solver status without trust story | Feasible witness plus independently auditable upper-bound evidence |
| Memory engineering | No estimate | Coarse estimate | Variables/constraints or state volume tied to a 48 GB mitigation plan |
| Failure analysis | Generic risks | Benchmark-specific risks | Specific failure signals, containment, and recovery without weakening soundness |

## Classification of Differences

- **Independent convergence:** same decision criterion despite different vocabulary.
- **Equivalent reformulation:** same logical guarantee with a different layer split.
- **Specification miss:** answer relies on an assumption contradicted by the external package.
- **Specification ambiguity:** answer exposes a genuinely unstated rule; escalate to the owner.
- **New candidate:** internally coherent alternative not represented in the current methodology; retain for separate evaluation.
- **Unsound shortcut:** improves search but can discard a legal layout or overstate proof authority.

Do not reward use of familiar terminology by itself. The key comparison is whether the response independently derives sound ownership, feedback, escalation, and audit criteria.
